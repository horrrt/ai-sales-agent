# Positioner hill-climb

Models: positioner `claude-opus-5-5`, judge `claude-sonnet-5-5`, proposer `claude-opus-5-5`.
1 samples per lead. Train: L-101, L-102, L-103, L-105, L-106, L-107, L-109, L-110, L-111. Holdout: L-104, L-108, L-112.
Accept rule: score up by more than 0.02, valid-angle rate down by at most 2%.

**Start:** 0.917 (valid 100%, n=12, errors 0), holdout 1.000 (valid 100%, n=3, errors 0)  
**End:** 0.917 (valid 100%, n=12, errors 0), holdout 1.000 (valid 100%, n=3, errors 0)

| Step | Old | New | Score before → after | Holdout before → after | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 |    - Every claim about the lead traces to a fact in `research.facts`. |    - Every claim about the lead traces to a fact in `research.facts`. Anything you infer beyond the fact (what their buyers ask for, who handles it, what they need next) is worded as a likely situation ("likely", "often", "a sign that"), never stated as something the lead is doing or facing. Leave out facts that don't support the chosen trigger. | 0.917 → 0.833 | 1.000 → 0.833 | rejected: score 0.917 -> 0.833 (needs > +0.02) |

Rationale per step:

1. Both weak angles rest on a fact that only shows who the lead sells to. They then state inferred consequences as facts: that those buyers send questionnaires, expect ISO 27001, or that the CEO answers them. The good angles frame the same kind of inference as a sign or a tendency. The reviewer marks angles weak when they assert inferences the research doesn't show, and it softens its grade when the inference is hedged, as in the good outputs. Requiring hedged wording and dropping tangential facts (like an unrelated certification used as a hook) should move inferred-trigger angles from weak toward good. It doesn't relax any evidence or brief rule, and angles that are already good keep their framing.
